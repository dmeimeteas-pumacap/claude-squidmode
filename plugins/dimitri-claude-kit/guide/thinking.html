△ Thinking

Three tools for not fooling yourself. Two of them push back on your ideas and differ mainly in when they arrive: /grill-me while the plan is still soft, /scrutinize once it has hardened. The third isn't a command at all.

/grill-me

Get interrogated about a plan or design until it actually holds together.

/grill-me
Takes no arguments — describe the plan in the conversation and it starts asking.

How it behaves

One question at a time, then it stops and waits. It works down the branches of the decision tree rather than asking everything at once, and it raises a concern at the moment it appears instead of saving up a critique for the end.

Why one at a time matters

A wall of ten questions gets three answered. Worse, a concern held back until you've committed to a direction arrives too late to change anything.

Reach for it when

You have a rough idea and a suspicion it has holes you can't see. Also good when you're about to build something big and want the assumptions named out loud first.

When NOT to

Not for a finished plan you want judged — that is /scrutinize, which runs a critic blind to your reasoning. Use this while the plan is still soft; once it has hardened, interrogation just re-litigates.

It treats your plan as a hypothesis, not a proposal to approve. If you want agreement rather than pressure, this is the wrong command.

/scrutinize

Independent adversarial critique of anything you've settled on — a plan, a product, a project, a claim, a document.

/scrutinize
Default critique.
--lens premortem
Assume it already failed, then work backwards to why.
--lens devils-advocate
Argue the opposing case as strongly as possible.
--lens multi-lens
Several stakeholder angles at once.
--depth rough | general | specific | implementation
How deep findings are allowed to go.

The critic is blind on purpose

It never sees the conversation that produced the thing, so it cannot be talked into agreeing and it has no sunk cost in the idea. That blindness is the whole mechanism.

Verdicts

PROCEED · PROCEED-WITH-FIXES · REWORK, with findings split into blocking and advisory. With-fixes is the normal resting result, not a failure.

The depth ladder

Cumulative. Declaring rough means implementation-level nitpicks are out of scope, which is what makes a clean PROCEED reachable at an early stage instead of an infinite critique loop.

Adding scope to a plan invalidates a previous verdict at that depth. Adding detail does not.

Not for

Hunting bugs in written code. This operates at the level of reasoning and direction.

Budget it

One to three passes is normal. Past round three, stop and decide rather than looping — an endless critique cycle is a way of not shipping.

/change-review always on

The standing format for how Claude shows you code edits. There is nothing to run.

—
Takes no arguments; it is not invoked. A standing format, always in effect.

What you get

A summary map first — one line per file, what changed and why — then per-file diffs with enough surrounding context to judge them. Small changes shown whole; large ones show the first significant hunk and offer the rest.

Why it is a skill

So the format is consistent and so subagents produce it directly rather than needing a second pass to reformat. It applies whether or not you think about it.

If you see it in a list

It looks like a command and isn't. Typing it does nothing useful. It is listed only so you know why edits arrive looking the way they do.

Worth knowing

Subagents are told to produce this format directly, so a delegated edit comes back reviewable without a second pass.
